Policy Background: why thermal power became a "dividend stock"

The macro premise behind the whole strategy: policy turned thermal power from a cyclical stock into a dividend / utility stock. This decides how drawdowns are later classified as A or B.

Core thesis: three constraints on thermal power

Costs are capped: long-term contract coal price kept in a 570–770 RMB/t "green band" (NDRC Doc. 303) → fewer coal-price shocks
Tariffs are boxed in: market reform allows ±20% floating (NDRC Doc. 1439) → tariffs can rise or fall, but elasticity is limited
Market share is squeezed: wind and solar get grid priority and installations keep beating plans → thermal generation space shrinks permanently
→ Conclusion: thermal power moved from "a cyclical stock earning the coal-to-power spread" to "a dividend stock living on stable cash flow and payouts"

Policy timeline (benchmark for factor checks)

Three Five-Year Plans: the last two beat their targets (tap a card for target vs. actual)

The 13th and 14th Five-Year Plans both beat their targets → the 15th plan's "coal power becomes a supporting, flexible source" is credible enough to use as the base case.

From policy to factor choices (directional hypotheses)

These are hypotheses extrapolated from policy: down-weight coal-price factors (capped), repurpose tariff and renewables-squeeze factors as an "A-permanent detector", and favour dividend / cash-flow factors.

Thermal Funnel: a big drop ≠ a wrong drop

Built step by step from real events: both are deep drawdowns, so how do you tell a mispricing (should rebound) from a value trap (deserved to fall)?

Start with two real events (where the funnel begins)

Case 1: Huaneng, Oct 2022, −16.4% relative

On the surface: the policy backdrop looked poor and the stock fell hard. It looked "deserved".

The actual data: tariffs +18% (power shortage), coal flat, a dry hydro year meant less squeeze → fundamentals were fine!

Verdict: Type B · Mispriced → wrong drop, should rebound → t+60 +7.3% ✅ recovered

Case 2: Huaneng, Jul 2024, −17.8% relative

On the surface: the stock got cheap and looked like a bargain.

The actual data: tariffs −2.85% (falling) + heavy renewables squeeze + a wet hydro year (+10.5%) taking more share → fundamentals really got worse!

Verdict: Type A · Permanent → value genuinely reset lower, stay away → t+60 −10.7% ❌ kept falling (trap)

What the two cases teach: the size of the drop alone tells you nothing. You have to check fundamentals (coal, tariffs, renewables, hydro) to judge whether the drop was deserved. That is the A/B attribution.

The 4-layer funnel built from these cases (tap to expand)

Screen: the thermal index's 60-day excess return vs. CSI 300 falls to or below its historical 5th percentile (threshold −16%) → trigger.
Why: excess vs. CSI 300 removes market-wide sell-offs; a historical percentile adapts on its own instead of a hand-picked threshold. Price only, no factors → avoids circular reasoning.
Result: 7 events in 10 years.
Inputs: high dividend yield, high FCF yield, low P/B: names that fell into value.
Frequency: annual financials ÷ daily price → a daily ratio signal.
No look-ahead: financials are lagged to their announcement date (an annual report is only public in April of the next year).
Apply the A/B attribution from the two cases:
· Type A · Permanent falling tariffs / renewables squeeze (e.g. 2024) → value truly lower → drop it (trap)
· Type A · Cyclical coal-price shock (e.g. 2020) → mean-reverts → check where the cycle is
· Type B · Mispriced fundamentals intact (e.g. 2022, tariffs +18%) → keep it (real opportunity)
Why it matters: "buy what's cheap" in layer 2 walks into traps, because a permanently worse stock keeps getting "cheaper". This layer removes them.
Score: composite = zscore(FCF yield) + zscore(prior resilience); hold the top 5 equal-weight for 60 days.
Why these two: they ranked best in the backtest (high-FCF mean +6.2%, prior resilience had the highest RankIC).
⚠ But the medians are weak (see "mean vs. median" below).
Each layer narrows the list down to the final picks

Hydro Funnel: swap "coal price" for "water inflow"

Hydro has no fuel cost; its "fuel" is water inflow. Same A/B framework: falling in a wet year = mispriced, falling in a dry year = a real drop.

Start with two real events

Case 1: hydro drawdown, Oct 2024

Check inflow: 2024 was wet (national hydro output +10.5%) → good fundamentals yet falling = mispriced

Verdict: Type B · Mispriced → t+60 +5.0% ✅ recovered

Case 2: hydro drawdown, Jul 2025

Check inflow: Q3 2025 was dry, −8%, quarterly output down 10–17% → genuinely weak = real drop

Verdict: Type A · Cyclical → t+60 −13.8% ❌ no recovery (buying in a dry year = trap)

The core rule for hydro: whether a hydro drawdown is buyable depends on whether it happens in a wet or dry period. Wet-year drop = mispriced (buy); dry-year drop = real (avoid). This is the thermal A/B framework with "coal" replaced by "water".

Hydro funnel (tap to expand)

An equal-weight index of 11 pure hydro names; 60-day excess vs. CSI 300 ≤ historical 5th percentile (threshold −14.4%) → trigger. 6 events since 2018.
Hydro has no coal; its fuel is water. Check the year's inflow / hydro output YoY: more water = more output = positive; dry = negative.
· Type B · Mispriced falling in a wet year (e.g. 2024, +10.5%) → good fundamentals → recovers (buy)
· Type A · Cyclical falling in a dry year (e.g. Q3 2025, −8%) → genuinely weak → no recovery (avoid)
The −13.8% loss in 2025 was exactly the "buy the dip in a dry year" trap.
Hydro names are homogeneous (all stable cash flow + high payouts) → stock selection doesn't work. Time the entry and pick the leader: of 6 hydro names tested, only Yangtze Power had a genuinely significant drawdown signal (+5.3%, p=0.03).
